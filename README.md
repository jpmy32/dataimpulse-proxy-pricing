## What DataImpulse proxy is, briefly

DataImpulse launched at the end of 2022 as part of the Softoria group, the Ukrainian IT company that also runs DataForSEO and ZoogVPN. The residential pool is built from its own bandwidth-sharing app rather than resold aggregator traffic, and that distinction matters more than the marketing copy suggests: IPs that only one provider sells carry less accumulated abuse history, which usually means fewer blocks on protected targets.

The commercial pitch is a flat rate. Residential traffic runs $1 per GB on a pay-as-you-go model, there's no subscription, and the gigabytes you buy don't expire. Buy 50 GB in March, burn 8 GB, and the other 42 sit in your balance until you use them. The minimum top-up is $5.

Alongside the standard residential pool, DataImpulse sells three other proxy types from the same account:

- **Residential** — 90M+ IPs across 195 locations, rotating or sticky sessions, HTTP(S) and SOCKS5, at $1/GB.
- **Premium residential** — a filtered, faster sub-pool at $5/GB, with all targeting options included and a dedicated account manager.
- **Datacenter** — 99.9% uptime, randomized subnets, sub-100 ms response times, at $0.50/GB. Cheapest and fastest, and the wrong tool for anti-bot-heavy sites.
- **Mobile** — real 3G/4G/5G/LTE carrier IPs at $2/GB, aimed at social platforms and app-side data where mobile fingerprints pass more easily.

## The non-expiring balance is the part that actually changes your budget

Monthly proxy bills usually assume steady consumption. Real scraping schedules don't work that way — you hammer a project for three weeks, then a month passes with almost nothing. On a subscription, that idle month is paid for and wasted. On prepaid credits with an expiry date, it's worse.

DataImpulse's balance simply sits there. For intermittent workloads that's a structural advantage rather than a nice-to-have, because your effective cost per successful request depends on how much of what you bought you actually get to spend. Just don't read it as "cheaper than everyone": if you genuinely burn 500 GB every month, the discount tiers matter far more than the expiry policy.

## Free versus paid targeting

Country-level targeting costs nothing. State, city, ZIP and ASN selection is a paid add-on, and DataImpulse's own FAQ describes it only as "a small extra fee." Third-party write-ups have been more specific, reporting that traffic routed through advanced filters is billed at roughly double the standard per-GB rate on residential plans, while datacenter plans appear to include those filters at no extra cost.

That spread is worth checking with support before you build a cost model around hyper-local targeting, because 2× on a $1/GB plan quietly turns into $2/GB. It's the single most common way people end up surprised by their first invoice.

## Getting credentials into your code

Setup lives entirely in the dashboard. You create an endpoint, pick rotating or sticky, choose a protocol and a target country, and get a username/password plus a gateway host. Two ports do most of the work:

- `823` — HTTP/HTTPS rotating, a fresh IP per request
- `824` — SOCKS5 rotating
- `10000–20000` — sticky sessions, where the IP stays bound to a port for a set interval

The username carries the targeting, which is why you don't need a separate dashboard entry per location. A US rotating residential request looks roughly like this:

python
import requests

proxy = "http://YOUR_LOGIN__cr.us:YOUR_PASSWORD@gw.dataimpulse.com:823"
r = requests.get("https://httpbin.org/ip", proxies={"http": proxy, "https": proxy})
print(r.json())


Sticky sessions default to 30 minutes if you don't specify an interval, and published ranges go up to 120 minutes. If a workflow depends on long-lived sessions, that ceiling is a real limitation — some competitors advertise sticky windows measured in hours or days, and DataImpulse doesn't. Rotating is the default for a reason: most scraping is stateless.

## Where it doesn't hold up

Four honest limits:

1. **Pool depth.** 90M+ IPs is mid-tier. Bright Data (400M+) and Oxylabs (175M+) still lead on the hardest targets and in thin geographies.
2. **No managed scraping layer.** There's no web scraping API, no CAPTCHA handling, no parsing. You get raw proxy connections and you write the rest. That's a feature if you already have a stack and a cost if you don't.
3. **Session length.** Sticky sessions cap out well below providers that advertise multi-hour or multi-day windows.
4. **Compliance paperwork.** One review notes the absence of SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certification, which stops procurement-gated enterprise deals cold regardless of price.
